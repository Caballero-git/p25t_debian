* DONE USB wireless keyboard/mouse receiver (host-mode test, patches 0007+0008)
  - Bluetooth keyboard turned out faulty (misbehaves on the PC too, not
    our stack). Built a second DTB, ~dr_mode = "host"~ instead of
    ~"peripheral"~ on the USB-C port, to try a USB receiver instead.
  - Swap in/out with ~usbhost_toggle.sh~ (see [[file:debian.org][debian]]).
    Sacrifices the gadget console + ~usb0~ while active; use Wi-Fi/SSH.
  - Result (2026-09-26): works. Needed two more fixes beyond the DTB swap,
    both now folded in permanently:
    1. ~CONFIG_USB_XHCI_HCD~/~CONFIG_USB_XHCI_PLATFORM~ were off in the
       kernel config - ~dwc3~ bound but nothing could drive the xHCI
       controller it created. Root cause found by reading our own
       ~.config~, not by guessing.
    2. No VBUS on the port at all - traced to RK817's ~BOOST~/~OTG_SWITCH~
       regulators being entirely absent from our DT. Added a minimal RK817
       node (patch 0008) with just these two, values copied from the stock
       DT, ~OTG_SWITCH~ wired to ~usb2phy0_otg~ via ~phy-supply~ only in
       the host-mode DTB. See [[file:hardware.org][hardware]] and
       [[file:kernel.org][kernel]] for the full writeup.
    Confirmed with both a wired USB keyboard and a Logitech USB receiver
    (keyboard + mouse) - both enumerate, ~input:~ devices register, typing
    works on the console.
  - Still open: this is a manual DTB swap, not automatic role switching -
    keeping host mode means giving up the gadget console/~usb0~ while it's
    active. Real OTG dual-role (see the USB gadget item below) would let
    the port switch automatically instead.

* DONE Battery and charger (RK817)
  - Patch 0008: minimal RK817 node, just ~BOOST~ and ~OTG_SWITCH~, for
    USB-C VBUS (see the USB wireless-receiver item above).
  - Patch 0009 (2026-09-26): added the ~charger {}~/~battery {}~ pair
    (~rk817_charger~, 5300 mAh, sense 10 mΩ, OCV table, all from the stock
    DT). Confirmed on hardware, cable power: ~rk817-battery~ and
    ~rk817-charger~ both probe, ~Charging~/~Standard~, ≈1.10 A, USB type
    ~DCP~. See [[file:hardware.org][hardware]] and [[file:kernel.org][kernel]].
  - Power key and RTC came online for free once the RK817 node existed at
    all (no charger-specific DT needed) — ~/dev/rtc0~ live and correct,
    input event devices present for the power key.
  - Every RK817 write was proposed and approved first, per the standing
    rule. Values mirror the stock DT; nothing that was on got switched off.
  - Follow-up from the battery-consumption review, resolved (patch 0010,
    2026-09-26): ~BOOST~ had been ~regulator-always-on~ in *every* DTB
    (patch 0008), not just host-mode. Moved the always-on/boot-on lines
    into a host-mode-only DTB override (same pattern as
    ~usb2phy0_otg { phy-supply = ...; }~); the host-mode DTB compiled
    byte-for-byte identical to before (verified by checksum), so zero
    regression risk on the receiver test. Confirmed on hardware: ~boost~
    now shows ~disabled~ in normal mode; charger/battery unaffected.
  - Also noted: the mainline ~rk817_charger.c~ driver hardcodes the USB
    input-current limit to 1.5 A (stock Android uses 2 A) and never
    touches the chip's own charge-enable bit (left at hardware default) —
    neither is a DT change, both would need a driver patch; not planned
    for now.
  - The "many hours, only 25%" report (2026-09-26) that triggered this
    review: the tablet had sat unused for months and started at a literal
    0% — something the user says they'd rarely seen before. A deeply
    discharged, long-idle Li-ion cell is routinely put through a slow
    pre-charge/trickle phase by the charger IC before ramping to full
    current, and the gas gauge's own calibration (relax voltage, OCV
    table) goes stale over months of no charge cycles. Both fit the
    symptom on their own, with nothing pointing at our kernel/DT changes;
    our own check minutes later (≈1.10 A, rising %) looks like normal
    charging past that initial phase. No further action - let it finish a
    full charge/rest cycle, which also lets the fuel gauge recalibrate.

* DONE CPU frequency (vdd_cpu / DVFS), TODO GPU
  - Flagged 2026-09-26 (battery-consumption review): with no cpufreq/OPPs
    at all, the CPU likely never dropped below its default/boot frequency —
    the most plausible *software* contributor to faster-than-Android
    drain during real use, bigger than anything found on the RK817 side.
  - Turned out frequency scaling was already silently active before any
    DT work here - ~rk3566.dtsi~ already ships a standard OPP table wired
    to all 4 CPUs, and ~CONFIG_CPU_FREQ~/~CPUFREQ_DT~/~CPUFREQ_DT_PLATDEV~
    were already on. Only voltage scaling was missing.
  - Patch 0011 (2026-09-26): wired the real ~vdd_cpu~ chip, SYR837
    (~silergy,syr827~ @ i2c0 0x40, confirmed from the stock DT - which
    lists four alternative vdd_cpu parts, only this one populated here)
    as ~cpu-supply~ on all 4 CPUs. Values copied verbatim from the stock
    DT. Every write proposed and approved first, per the standing rule -
    same as the RK817 patches, even though this chip isn't RK817, since
    it's the CPU core rail. See [[file:hardware.org][hardware]] and
    [[file:kernel.org][kernel]] for the full writeup.
  - Confirmed working 2026-09-26: clean hardware detect in ~dmesg~, no
    errors; voltage tracks frequency exactly against the OPP table's
    targets (1.025 V at 1416 MHz idle, 1.15 V at 1800 MHz under load -
    both exact matches, not just in-range).
  - Still open: ~vdd_gpu~ (RK817 DCDC) for Panfrost - GPU has an OPP table
    too (~rk3566.dtsi~) but no supply wired, same situation CPU was in
    before this patch. Not started.

* OPEN Power button doesn't wake it from off unless USB cable is plugged in
  - Found 2026-09-25, reported 2026-09-26 (unrelated to the USB host-mode
    DTB, patch 0007): with the tablet fully off and no cable, pressing the
    power button does nothing. Plugging a USB-C cable into the PC makes it
    power on by itself, no button press needed.
  - Very likely NOT a software issue on our side. The decision to power on
    from a fully-off state is made by the RK817 PMIC's own hardware state
    machine (ONKEY debounce, VBUS-presence auto-boot), *before* the SoC
    boot ROM runs — i.e. before U-Boot, our kernel, our DTB or the
    initramfs get to execute at all. We have never written to the RK817 on
    the real hardware (only read-only dumps, plus the "rails" LDO
    experiment which was only ever exercised in an emulated PMIC, never on
    the tablet) - see [[file:findings.org][findings]].
  - Auto-boot-on-VBUS is a common, intentional PMIC/charger feature on
    Android devices and is probably factory behavior, not something we
    configured.
  - Best isolation test (cheap, no risk): remove the SD card so the
    tablet boots stock Android, and try the power button with no cable.
    If it also fails to turn on, this is stock hardware/firmware
    behavior, nothing to do with this project.
  - Possible causes if it turns out to be real: aged/near-empty battery
    (the 4.28 V reading was taken while charging, not at rest), a
    battery-protection cutoff that needs VBUS to release, or the power
    button itself needing a longer hold than usual.
  - Supporting data point (2026-09-26): after months of disuse the tablet
    was found at a literal 0%, unusually low for it - consistent with a
    battery-protection cutoff and/or a long-idle self-discharge, either of
    which would need VBUS to release/recover before the ONKEY press can
    do anything. Strengthens the "stock hardware behavior on this
    particular, long-idle battery" reading over a software cause.
  - Practical workaround for testing (e.g. the USB host-mode DTB, which
    needs no PC cable once running): power on with the PC cable plugged
    in as usual, wait for it to boot, then unplug the PC and plug in the
    USB receiver instead - the DWC3 host controller enumerates whatever
    is connected at the time, so it doesn't need to be the receiver at
    the moment of power-on.
  - Do not attempt a fix by writing RK817 registers without proposing it
    first - this touches power-on/charging logic, not just a display or
    Wi-Fi rail.
